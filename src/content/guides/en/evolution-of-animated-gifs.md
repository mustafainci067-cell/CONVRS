## Introduction

The GIF was introduced by CompuServe in 1987. Despite its age and inefficiency, it remains a pillar of internet culture.

## Technological Limitations

GIFs are limited to a 256-color palette. They do not support true alpha channel transparency, only binary transparency, leading to jagged edges.

## Why the GIF Survived

Universal browser support and the rise of meme culture kept the GIF alive. It's the easiest way to share a silent, looping video clip across platforms.

## Modern Alternatives

Today, formats like WebP, AVIF, and silent looping MP4s (often disguised as GIFs by platforms like Twitter) offer vastly superior performance and quality.
