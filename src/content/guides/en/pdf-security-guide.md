## Introduction to PDF Security

PDFs are the standard for document sharing in the professional world. However, sharing sensitive information without proper security measures can lead to catastrophic data leaks. In this guide, we will break down the different layers of PDF security.

## Encryption Levels

Modern PDFs use strong encryption algorithms like AES-256. When a document is encrypted, its contents are mathematically scrambled and cannot be read without the correct decryption key.

## User Password vs. Owner Password

A 'User Password' (Document Open Password) is required just to open and view the PDF. An 'Owner Password' (Permissions Password) allows anyone to open the document, but restricts certain actions like printing, copying text, or editing.

## Why Local Processing Matters

When you use an online tool to add or remove PDF passwords, you are trusting the server with your document. This is why our tools use zero-backend client-side processing, ensuring your data never leaves your device.

## Redaction: The Right Way

Drawing a black box over sensitive text is not redaction; anyone can delete the box. Proper redaction completely removes the underlying text data from the file.

## Digital Signatures

Digital signatures mathematically prove that a document has not been altered since it was signed, providing non-repudiation.

## Conclusion

Take control of your document security. Always use strong passwords, proper redaction, and client-side tools.
